# My Writing Practice

> Starting draft, written with AI assistance (Claude) at my request. I will rewrite this in my own words and
> keep this note until I have. Policy details (university, advisor, journals, IRB) are mine to look up; nothing
> below is a claim about what any of them require.

**Why:** build a writing voice I can reproduce on demand and defend, and keep a visible process while I do it.

## Three rules

1. **First drafts are mine.** Written by me, without AI, as bad as they need to be.
2. **Nothing is overwritten.** Every draft is a new, dated file; history is kept and pushed.
3. **Small and daily beats big and rare.** A 10-minute session counts.

## Daily drafting block (30-45 minutes)

- Same time every day, **before email**. One topic. Timer on.
- Open the project (`jwrite <project>` or `focus`), create a **new file**: `drafts\YYYY-MM-DD-topic.md`.
- Draft only. No editing, no looking things up, no AI. Stop when the timer ends.
- Note the word count. The number is for the habit, not for judging the writing.

## Edit day (a different day from the draft)

- Edit a **copy**: `YYYY-MM-DD-topic-edit1.md`. Keep the original.
- Three separate passes: (1) the argument, (2) the sentences (read aloud), (3) the citations.
- Cut more than I add.

## Weekly cold write (25 minutes, 300 words)

- A prompt, no sources, no AI, saved as `cold-writes\YYYY-MM-DD.md`.
- Prompt ideas: summarise an article I read from memory; explain my dissertation argument to a smart non-specialist;
  describe one scene from fieldwork; defend a claim I disagree with.
- Once a month, reread the last four. This is my baseline for how I actually write, and my record of growth.

## Reading (1-2 hours a week, on purpose)

- Read for sentences, not just content. Keep a short file of **moves I like** (how a paragraph opens, how a claim
  is hedged) and borrow the *move*, never the words.

## Feedback from people

- Advisor, writing group, writing center. Bring a rough draft early. Log what they said and what I changed.

## How I use AI (my rules; check them against the actual policies)

**Okay for me:** questioning my ideas, finding gaps in my argument, explaining a concept, giving feedback on a
*finished* draft I wrote, code and tooling, formatting.
**Not for me:** producing sentences I will submit, rewriting my prose, "humanizers" or detector-evasion tools,
or trusting a citation I have not checked in the source.
**If unsure:** don't, or ask my advisor first.

- **Verify every citation** against the source. Track problems in `REFERENCE_PROBLEMS.md`.
- **Log it** in `ai-use-log.md` (template below). Disclose where a policy requires it.
- Policies to read once and summarise in my own words: UC Merced (research and teaching), my department and
  advisor, each journal/conference I target, my IRB protocol. Date the summary.

## Keeping the record

- End of each session: look at `gitchanges`, then `git add -A`, `git commit -m "Draft: <what I did>"`, `git push`.
- Never rewrite history. Keep recordings, transcripts and consent forms out of the repo.

## Weekly review (10 minutes, Friday)

Days I drafted: _ / 5. Words: _. Cold write done: yes / no. One thing that got easier: _. One thing to change: _.

## When it feels bad

Shrink the session to 10 minutes and still do it. A rough day is still a day of practice. Skipping once is not a
streak failure; skipping the next one is the thing to avoid.
